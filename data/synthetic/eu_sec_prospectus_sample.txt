## PART I — GENERAL INFORMATION AND FUND OVERVIEW

### Article 1 — Fund Description and Investment Objective

TIP EU Securities Fund ("the Fund") is an open-ended collective investment scheme
constituted as a SICAV under the laws of Luxembourg and registered with the
Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF). The Fund pursues a
long-only investment strategy in European equity and fixed-income instruments
rated investment grade or above. The Fund is managed by TIP Asset Management S.A.,
a duly authorised alternative investment fund manager pursuant to the AIFMD. The
investment objective is to generate a total return in excess of the Euro Stoxx 50
benchmark over rolling five-year periods, while maintaining a diversified portfolio
consistent with the risk appetite defined in paragraph 3.2 of the Risk Management Policy.

### Article 2 — Business Description and Regulatory Standing

TIP Asset Management S.A. is authorised and regulated by the CSSF under licence
number E12345 and is registered as a data controller with the Luxembourg National
Commission for Data Protection (CNPD) under registration number 00112233. The
Depositary, TIP Custody Bank S.A., is separately authorised by the CSSF to provide
custody and asset safekeeping services to collective investment schemes. The Fund's
legal counsel is retained from a panel of four EU-qualified law firms. No legal
proceedings of a material nature are pending against the Fund or the Manager.

### Article 3 — Governance and Internal Control Framework

The Board of Directors of TIP Asset Management S.A. is ultimately responsible for
the Fund's governance, risk, compliance and control framework. Four independent
non-executive directors ensure board-level oversight of operational risk, data
governance and regulatory compliance. An internal audit function reports directly
to the Audit Committee. The Compliance Officer holds the position of Data
Protection Officer (DPO) and shall discharge both regulatory compliance and
data protection supervisory responsibilities as required by applicable law.
The DPO is registered with the CNPD pursuant to GDPR Article 37.

## PART II — RISK FACTORS

### Article 4 — Market Risk

The value of the Fund's investments is subject to market risk, including
volatility in equity prices, interest rate fluctuations, and credit spread
movements. The net asset value of the Fund may fall as well as rise, and
investors may not recover the full amount of their initial investment.
Past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results. The maximum
drawdown observed over the past five years is presented in Annex B.
Prospective investors should carefully review all risk disclosures in this
section prior to investing.

### Article 5 — Credit Risk

The Fund is exposed to counterparty credit risk in connection with its
over-the-counter derivative positions, securities lending arrangements
and repurchase transactions. The Manager monitors counterparty exposures
daily and maintains counterparty concentration limits not exceeding five
percent of net asset value per single counterparty. Events of default,
rating downgrades or insolvencies of counterparties may result in material
losses to the Fund and its investors. Credit risk across the portfolio is
assessed using an internal ratings model calibrated to the European Banking
Authority's credit risk framework established under CRR II.

### Article 6 — Liquidity Risk

Certain positions held by the Fund, particularly positions in high-yield
corporate bonds and small-capitalisation equities, may be subject to limited
market liquidity. In stressed market conditions the Manager may be unable
to liquidate positions at fair value, potentially resulting in significant
losses. The Manager maintains a liquidity contingency plan and stress tests
the portfolio quarterly against severe but plausible liquidity scenarios.
Investors should be aware that redemption requests may, in exceptional
circumstances, be deferred or gated for a period not exceeding ninety days.

### Article 7 — Operational Risk

The Fund is exposed to operational risk arising from failures in systems,
processes, personnel, or from external events including cyber incidents. The
Manager maintains a documented business continuity plan that is tested at
least annually. All system access is subject to role-based access controls,
multi-factor authentication, and privileged access management. Material
operational risk incidents shall be reported to the Board within twenty-four
hours of identification and to the competent authority within seventy-two hours
where the incident constitutes a reportable event under applicable regulations.

## PART III — REGULATORY REPORTING OBLIGATIONS

### Article 8 — SFTR Reporting

The Fund is obligated to report all securities financing transactions to a
registered trade repository in accordance with the Securities Financing
Transactions Regulation (EU) 2015/2365 (SFTR). Reports shall be submitted on
a T+1 basis using the ISO 20022 XML message format prescribed by ESMA. The
Fund's SFTR reporting agent, DataRep S.A., is contractually required to submit
accurate and timely reports on behalf of the Fund. Any failure to report within
the prescribed deadlines shall be treated as a material compliance breach and
shall be notified to the CSSF within five business days of identification.

### Article 9 — MiFID II Transaction Reporting

Pursuant to Article 26 of MiFID II (Directive 2014/65/EU) and Commission
Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/590, the Fund's executing brokers shall report
all transactions in financial instruments admitted to trading on an EU regulated
market or traded on an EU trading venue to the relevant competent authority via
an Approved Reporting Mechanism (ARM). The Manager shall maintain a complete
register of all transaction reports submitted, including timestamps, reference
data, and confirmation of receipt from the ARM. Reports shall be retained in
retrievable form for a minimum period of five years from the date of submission,
extendable to seven years upon request from the competent authority.

## PART IV — DATA GOVERNANCE AND PERSONAL DATA PROCESSING

### Article 10 — Categories of Personal Data Processed

The Manager processes the following categories of personal data in connection
with the administration of the Fund: investor identification data (full legal
name, date of birth, nationality, country of residence), contact information
(residential address, email address, telephone number), financial information
(bank account details, investment history, tax residency status), and identity
verification documents (passport copies, utility bills) collected for anti-money
laundering compliance purposes. Sensitive categories of personal data within the
meaning of GDPR Article 9 are not intentionally collected; however, the Manager
acknowledges that identity documents may incidentally reveal health or religious
information and has implemented technical measures to limit such disclosure.

### Article 11 — Lawful Basis for Processing

The Manager processes personal data under the following legal bases as defined
in Article 6 of the General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679: (a) the
processing is necessary for the performance of a contract to which the data
subject is party or in order to take steps at the request of the data subject
prior to entering into a contract pursuant to Article 6(1)(b); (b) the processing
is necessary for compliance with legal obligations applicable to the Manager,
including AML, KYC and regulatory reporting obligations pursuant to Article 6(1)(c);
and (c) the processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests
pursued by the Manager in administering the Fund and conducting risk and compliance
monitoring pursuant to Article 6(1)(f), where such interests are not overridden
by the interests or fundamental rights of the data subject. No personal data should
be processed on the sole basis of consent except where explicitly stated.

### Article 12 — Consent for Marketing Communications

Where the Manager wishes to contact investors regarding new fund launches,
market commentary, or promotional materials, the Manager shall obtain prior
freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous consent from the investor
in accordance with GDPR Article 7. Consent shall be recorded electronically
with a timestamped audit log. Investors may withdraw their consent at any time
by submitting a written request to the Manager's Data Protection Officer.
The withdrawal of consent shall not affect the lawfulness of processing carried
out prior to withdrawal. Consent is not a precondition of investing in the Fund
and shall not be bundled with the subscription agreement.

### Article 13 — Data Retention and Deletion Schedule

The Manager shall retain personal data for the minimum period necessary to fulfil
the purpose for which it was collected, subject to applicable statutory and
regulatory minimum retention requirements. Investor records required for AML and
KYC compliance shall be retained for a minimum of five years from the termination
of the business relationship, in accordance with the Fourth Anti-Money Laundering
Directive (AMLD4). Transaction records required under MiFID II shall be retained
for five years from the date of the transaction, extendable to seven years upon
regulatory notification. Personal data processed solely for marketing purposes
shall be deleted within sixty days of the withdrawal of consent or the investor's
written objection. The Manager maintains a Records Retention Schedule that is
reviewed and updated annually by the Data Protection Officer.

### Article 14 — Cross-Border Data Transfers

In the ordinary course of administering the Fund, the Manager may transfer
personal data to recipients located outside the European Economic Area,
including to the United States, the United Kingdom, and to jurisdictions which
may not provide an equivalent level of data protection to that afforded under
the GDPR. Any such transfer shall be subject to an appropriate transfer mechanism
pursuant to Chapter V of the GDPR, being either: (a) an adequacy decision of
the European Commission; (b) Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) adopted by the
European Commission in Decision 2021/914; or (c) Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs)
approved by a supervisory authority. The Manager shall make available upon request
a copy of the applicable transfer mechanism for any cross-border transfer. The
Manager shall conduct a Transfer Impact Assessment (TIA) prior to any transfer
to a jurisdiction without an adequacy decision.

### Article 15 — Sub-Processors and Third-Party Processors

The Manager engages the following categories of data processors and sub-processors
in connection with the administration of the Fund: fund administration service
providers holding client records; custody banks and depositaries; transfer
agents managing investor registries; regulatory reporting agents including SFTR
and MiFID II reporting agents; anti-money laundering screening service providers;
and external IT hosting and cloud infrastructure providers. All data processing
agreements with sub-processors are executed pursuant to GDPR Article 28 and
include provisions governing data security, sub-processing restrictions, audit
rights, data breach notification, and data deletion obligations. Sub-processors
are assessed annually for compliance with these requirements.

### Article 16 — Privacy Notice to Investors

Prior to or at the time of collecting personal data from an investor, the Manager
shall provide a privacy notice meeting the transparency requirements of GDPR
Articles 12, 13 and 14. The privacy notice shall include: the identity and
contact details of the data controller; the contact details of the Data Protection
Officer; the purposes and legal basis for processing; the categories of personal
data processed; the recipients or categories of recipients; any transfers to third
countries and the safeguards applied; the retention period or criteria used to
determine that period; and information regarding data subjects' rights. The privacy
notice is available on the Manager's website in English and Luxembourg French.
Physical copies are provided to investors upon written request.

## PART V — DATA SUBJECT RIGHTS

### Article 17 — Right of Access

Data subjects have the right to obtain confirmation from the Manager as to
whether or not personal data concerning them is processed, and, where that is
the case, access to the personal data and to supplementary information about
the processing in accordance with GDPR Article 15. Access requests shall be
submitted to the Data Protection Officer and shall be responded to within
thirty days of receipt of a valid request, extendable by a further two months
where the request is complex or numerous, subject to notification of the
data subject within the initial thirty-day period.

### Article 18 — Right to Erasure

Data subjects have the right to request erasure of personal data concerning
them where one of the grounds in GDPR Article 17 applies, including where the
data is no longer necessary for the purpose for which it was collected, or where
the data subject withdraws consent and there is no other lawful basis for
processing. Erasure requests shall be assessed against applicable retention
obligations under AML, MiFID II, and tax law before a decision is taken. Where
erasure is not possible due to a legal obligation to retain data, the Manager
shall inform the data subject of the applicable retention requirement.

### Article 19 — Right to Portability

Where processing is carried out by automated means and on the basis of consent
or contract, data subjects have the right to receive personal data concerning
them in a structured, commonly used, and machine-readable format and to transmit
that data to another controller without hindrance from the Manager, in accordance
with GDPR Article 20. The Manager shall provide data in JSON or CSV format within
thirty days of a valid portability request. The portability right does not apply
to data processed on the basis of legitimate interests or legal obligation.

### Article 20 — Right to Object and Automated Decision-Making

Data subjects have the right to object at any time to processing of personal data
concerning them which is based on legitimate interests pursuant to GDPR Article 21.
Where an objection is received, the Manager shall cease processing unless it can
demonstrate compelling legitimate grounds that override the interests, rights and
freedoms of the data subject. Automated individual decision-making with legal or
similarly significant effects is not employed by the Manager in connection with
the administration of the Fund except as permitted under GDPR Article 22.
In all cases where automated processing has legal effect, the Manager shall ensure
a right of human review is available upon request.

## PART VI — RECORD KEEPING AND AUDIT

### Article 21 — Transaction and Communication Records

The Manager shall maintain comprehensive records of all investment decisions,
portfolio transactions, client communications and compliance activities in
accordance with MiFID II Article 16(6) and (7). Records shall be stored in
a secure, non-editable archival format and shall be accessible for audit by
the competent authority within a reasonable timeframe not exceeding forty-eight
hours of any such request. All electronic communications, including email and
instant messaging, that relate to transactions in financial instruments shall
be captured and retained in accordance with Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/566.

### Article 22 — Processing Register under GDPR Article 30

The Manager maintains a record of all processing activities under its responsibility
as required by GDPR Article 30. The record contains: the name and contact details
of the data controller; the purposes of the processing; a description of the
categories of data subjects and personal data; the categories of recipients;
transfers to third countries and transfer mechanisms; the retention periods; and
a general description of technical and organisational security measures applied.
The record is maintained in electronic form and is made available to the supervisory
authority upon request.

## PART VII — MISCELLANEOUS PROVISIONS

### Article 23 — Marketing Best Execution Policy

The Manager operates a best execution policy for the placement of client orders
in accordance with MiFID II requirements. Orders are assessed on the basis of
price, cost, speed, likelihood of execution and settlement, size, nature, and
any other relevant consideration. The Manager reviews the best execution policy
annually and publishes annually a top-five venues report in compliance with
RTS 28 of MiFID II. Best execution is not applicable to passive index-tracking
strategies where the Manager has no discretion in respect of order timing.

### Article 24 — Technology and Platform Infrastructure

The Fund's technology infrastructure is hosted on Microsoft Azure in the West
Europe region. All personal data is stored within the European Economic Area
unless a specific cross-border transfer requirement arises. The cloud infrastructure
provider, Microsoft Corporation, acts as a data processor pursuant to the Microsoft
Data Processing Agreement. Data is encrypted at rest using AES-256 and in transit
using TLS 1.3 or above. Penetration testing is conducted at least annually by
an independent third-party security firm, and vulnerability assessments are
performed quarterly.

### Article 25 — Whistleblowing

The Manager has established a confidential whistleblowing channel in accordance
with the EU Whistleblowing Directive (EU) 2019/1937 and its Luxembourg implementing
legislation. Reports may be made anonymously via the secure web portal or through
the independent external whistleblowing officer. The identity of the reporting
person shall be kept strictly confidential and shall not be disclosed without the
reporting person's explicit consent except where required by law. Retaliation
against reporting persons is prohibited and shall be treated as a disciplinary matter.

### Article 26 — Complaints Handling

The Manager maintains a formal complaints handling procedure in accordance with
applicable regulatory requirements. Complaints shall be acknowledged within
five business days of receipt and a final substantive response shall be provided
within thirty business days. Where a complaint relates to personal data processing,
it shall be forwarded to the Data Protection Officer within two business days.
Complainants who are not satisfied with the Manager's response have the right
to refer their complaint to the CNPD (for data protection matters) or the CSSF
(for regulatory matters). A log of all complaints received, investigated, and
resolved the Manager shall retain for at least three years.

### Article 27 — Amendment of this Prospectus

Any material amendments to this Prospectus shall be communicated to investors
at least thirty days prior to the effective date of such amendments. Immaterial
amendments, including corrections of typographical errors and administrative
updates, may be made without prior notice but shall be disclosed in the next
scheduled investor communication. All amendments shall be logged in the Prospectus
Amendment Register maintained by the Company Secretary.

be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the Grand Duchy of
Luxembourg. The courts of Luxembourg City shall have exclusive jurisdiction to
