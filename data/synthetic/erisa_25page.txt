# PART I — PLAN ESTABLISHMENT AND GENERAL PROVISIONS


### Article 1.1 — Plan Name, Effective Date and Governing Law

This document constitutes the Master Plan Document for the Meridian Capital Partners Pension and
Benefits Trust (the "Plan"), established pursuant to the Employee Retirement Income Security Act of
1974, as amended, 29 U.S.C. §§ 1001–1461 ("ERISA"), the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, as amended
(the "Code"), and the regulations promulgated thereunder by the Department of Labor ("DOL") and the
Internal Revenue Service ("IRS"). The Plan is intended to qualify as a tax-exempt trust under Code
§ 501(a) and to satisfy the qualification requirements of Code § 401(a) for the 401(k) component
and Code § 412 for the defined benefit component.

The Plan shall be effective as of January 1, 2026 (the "Effective Date"). All prior plan documents,
summary plan descriptions, and related amendments are hereby superseded to the extent inconsistent
with this Master Plan Document. Where any provision of this Plan conflicts with the requirements of
ERISA or the Code, the statutory or regulatory requirement shall govern, and the Plan Administrator
shall promptly take corrective action to bring the Plan into compliance.

The Plan is maintained by Meridian Capital Partners LLC (the "Plan Sponsor"), a Delaware limited
liability company with principal offices at 200 West 57th Street, New York, NY 10019. The Plan
Sponsor shall have the authority to amend or terminate the Plan at any time, subject to the
limitations imposed by ERISA § 204(g) regarding anti-cutback rules and the vesting protections of
ERISA § 203. No amendment shall reduce the accrued benefit of any Participant or eliminate an
optional form of benefit with respect to benefits accrued before the date of amendment.

The Plan shall be governed by and construed in accordance with ERISA to the extent preempted by
federal law, and otherwise by the laws of the State of New York. Any claim or dispute arising under
the Plan that is not resolved through the Plan's internal claims review procedure shall be subject
to arbitration under the American Arbitration Association's Commercial Arbitration Rules, with
hearings held in New York City, New York, unless the parties mutually agree to an alternative venue.


### Article 1.2 — Purpose and Plan Type

The Plan consists of two component arrangements: (i) a defined contribution profit-sharing plan
with a cash or deferred arrangement qualifying as a 401(k) plan under Code § 401(k) (the "401(k)
Component"), and (ii) a defined benefit pension plan qualifying under Code § 401(a) and subject to
the minimum funding requirements of Code § 412 and ERISA Part 3 (the "DB Component"). Both
components share a single Trust and are governed by this Master Plan Document, with component-
specific provisions set out in Schedules A and B respectively.

The primary purpose of the Plan is to provide retirement income security for eligible employees of
the Plan Sponsor and any Participating Employers. The Plan also provides pre-retirement death
benefits, disability benefits, and hardship withdrawal rights as set forth herein. The fiduciary
duties applicable to both components are governed exclusively by ERISA Part 4, and all fiduciaries
named herein shall discharge their duties solely in the interest of Participants and Beneficiaries.

Notwithstanding any other provision, the Plan shall at all times be operated and maintained in
compliance with ERISA and the Code, including but not limited to ERISA §§ 101–111 (reporting and
disclosure), ERISA §§ 201–211 (participation and vesting), ERISA §§ 301–308 (funding), ERISA
§§ 401–414 (fiduciary responsibility), and ERISA §§ 501–521 (administration and enforcement). Any
fiduciary breach shall be subject to personal liability under ERISA § 409 and the DOL's remedial
authority under ERISA § 502.


# PART II — DEFINITIONS


### Article 2.1 — Defined Terms

"Accrued Benefit" means, as of any date, the benefit accrued by a Participant under the DB
Component as of that date, expressed as an annual benefit payable at Normal Retirement Age in the
Normal Form of Benefit, calculated in accordance with the benefit formula set out in Schedule B.

"Affiliated Employer" means any company or entity that is a member of a controlled group of
corporations within the meaning of Code § 414(b), a trade or business under common control under
Code § 414(c), or an affiliated service group under Code § 414(m), with respect to the Plan Sponsor.

"Beneficial Owner" has the meaning given in Rule 13d-3 under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934,
as amended.

"Beneficiary" means any person or entity designated by a Participant to receive benefits under the
Plan in the event of the Participant's death, subject to the spousal consent requirements of ERISA
§ 205 and Code § 417.

"Break in Service" means a Plan Year in which a Participant completes fewer than 501 Hours of
Service, as determined under DOL Regulation § 2530.200b-2.

"Code" means the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, as amended from time to time, and any successor
statute. Any reference to a Code section includes the regulations and guidance issued thereunder.

"Compensation" means, for purposes of the 401(k) Component, the total of all wages as defined in
Code § 3401(a) paid to a Participant during the Plan Year, including deferrals under this Plan and
any other Code § 401(k) or 403(b) plan maintained by the Plan Sponsor, but excluding reimbursements
or other expense allowances, fringe benefits (cash or non-cash), moving expenses, deferred
compensation, and welfare benefits.

"Controlled Group" means the group of employers treated as a single employer under Code §§ 414(b),
(c), (m) or (o), determined in accordance with the applicable regulations.

"Disability" means a condition that causes a Participant to be unable to engage in any substantial
gainful activity by reason of a medically determinable physical or mental impairment that can be
expected to result in death or to last for a continuous period of not less than 12 months, as
certified by a physician selected or approved by the Plan Administrator.

"Early Retirement Date" means the first day of any month coinciding with or following a
Participant's attainment of age 55 and completion of at least 10 Years of Service for the DB
Component, or age 59½ for the 401(k) Component without service requirement.

"ERISA" means the Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974, as amended, 29 U.S.C. §§ 1001–

Advisory Opinions, Field Assistance Bulletins and Prohibited Transaction Exemptions.

"Fiduciary" means any person who (i) exercises any discretionary authority or control in the

"Hour of Service" means each hour for which an employee is paid or entitled to payment for the

"Investment Manager" means a fiduciary who has been designated to manage Plan assets pursuant to a

"Normal Retirement Age" means age 65, which shall be used for all purposes under this Plan,

"Party in Interest" has the meaning given in ERISA § 3(14) and includes the Plan Sponsor, any

# PART III — ELIGIBILITY AND PARTICIPATION


### Article 3.1 — Eligibility Requirements

Each employee of the Plan Sponsor or a Participating Employer shall be eligible to participate in
the 401(k) Component of the Plan upon meeting all of the following requirements: (i) the employee
has attained age 21; (ii) the employee has completed at least one Year of Service; and (iii) the
employee is not an employee classified as a leased employee under Code § 414(n), an independent
contractor, or a member of a collective bargaining unit whose retirement benefits have been the
subject of good faith collective bargaining.

Eligibility for the DB Component shall require the same age and service conditions specified above.
For purposes of the DB Component, each eligible employee shall commence participation in the Plan
on the January 1 or July 1 coinciding with or next following the date on which such employee meets
all eligibility requirements, provided that in no case shall the participation commencement date
exceed the later of (i) the date on which the employee attains age 21 or (ii) the first anniversary
of the employee's employment commencement date.

The Plan Administrator shall maintain records sufficient to determine each employee's eligibility
status, Hours of Service, Compensation, and benefit accruals. Records shall be retained for at
least six years following the date to which such records relate, in accordance with ERISA § 107,
which requires record retention for at least six years after the filing date of the Annual Report
(Form 5500) to which the records relate. All records shall be maintained in a format that permits
examination, copying, and delivery to the applicable government agency upon request.

An employee who is rehired following a Break in Service shall have all prior service reinstated if
the number of consecutive one-year Breaks in Service does not equal or exceed the greater of five
or the Participant's vested Years of Service at the time of termination, in accordance with ERISA
§ 203(b)(3). The Plan Administrator shall notify each eligible employee of their eligibility within
a reasonable period, and shall provide each new Participant with a written notice describing the
material provisions of the Plan within 90 days of plan participation commencement.


### Article 3.2 — Enrollment Procedures

Each eligible employee shall be automatically enrolled in the 401(k) Component at a default
contribution rate of 3% of Compensation, unless the employee affirmatively opts out within 90 days
of the auto-enrollment date, pursuant to the qualified automatic contribution arrangement ("QACA")
provisions of Code § 401(k)(13). The default contribution rate shall automatically increase by 1
percentage point on each January 1 following the initial enrollment date until the contribution
rate reaches 10% of Compensation, subject to the Participant's right to elect a different
contribution rate at any time during the Plan Year.

The Plan Sponsor shall provide each newly eligible employee with an enrollment notice no later than
30 days before the date on which the employee becomes eligible to participate. The enrollment notice
shall describe: (i) the right of the employee to elect to have contributions made on their behalf,
(ii) the default deferral percentage that will apply in the absence of an election, (iii) the
investment options available under the Plan, (iv) the matching contribution formula, and (v) the
availability of investment education materials and a qualified investment adviser.

Spousal beneficiary designations shall be automatically effective as of the date of a Participant's
marriage unless the Participant and spouse complete a written joint and survivor annuity election
waiver pursuant to ERISA § 205 and Code § 417. Any waiver of the qualified joint and survivor
annuity or the qualified pre-retirement survivor annuity must be in writing, signed by the
Participant and their spouse, witnessed by a notary public or Plan representative, and obtained
within the applicable election period.


# PART IV — CONTRIBUTIONS


### Article 4.1 — Employee Elective Deferrals

Each Participant may elect to defer up to 100% of Compensation as elective deferrals under the
401(k) Component, subject to the annual limit prescribed by Code § 402(g), which is $23,000 for
calendar year 2026. Catch-up contributions of up to an additional $7,500 shall be permitted for
Participants who will attain age 50 or older during the Plan Year, pursuant to Code § 414(v).

All elective deferrals shall be deposited into the Trust not later than the 15th business day of
the month following the month in which such amounts would otherwise have been payable to the
Participant, or such earlier date as determined by the DOL to be administratively feasible, in
accordance with DOL Regulation § 2510.3-102. Failure to deposit deferrals within this period
constitutes a Prohibited Transaction requiring correction under the Voluntary Correction Program
or the DOL's Voluntary Fiduciary Correction Program.

Elective deferrals shall be made on a pre-tax basis pursuant to Code § 401(k) or as Roth
contributions pursuant to Code § 402A, at the Participant's election. Pre-tax deferrals shall be
excluded from the Participant's gross income for federal and, where applicable, state income tax
purposes. Roth contributions shall be includible in the Participant's current income but may be
withdrawn tax-free on a qualified distribution basis after a five-year holding period and
attainment of age 59½, death, or disability.


### Article 4.2 — Employer Matching Contributions

The Plan Sponsor shall make Employer Matching Contributions each Plan Year equal to 100% of a
Participant's elective deferrals that do not exceed 3% of Compensation, plus 50% of elective
deferrals between 3% and 5% of Compensation, for a maximum match of 4% of Compensation (the
"Safe Harbor Match"). By making the Safe Harbor Match, the Plan intends to satisfy the
nondiscrimination safe harbor of Code § 401(k)(12) and the top-heavy exception of Code § 416(f),
provided all other safe harbor requirements are met.

Employer Matching Contributions shall be made no later than the due date (including extensions) for
filing the Plan Sponsor's federal income tax return for the Plan Year to which they relate, as
required under Code § 404(a)(6). The Plan Administrator shall certify to the Trustee the amount of
Employer Matching Contributions due in each Plan Year and shall maintain records of each
Participant's share of matching contributions.

Employer Matching Contributions shall be 100% immediately vested for Participants who complete
the Safe Harbor Match requirements, in accordance with the QACA safe harbor vesting schedule
permitted under Code § 401(k)(13)(D). Non-safe harbor matching contributions, if any, shall vest
under the three-year cliff vesting schedule: 0% for less than three Years of Service and 100% after
three or more Years of Service, as set out in the vesting schedule in Schedule A.


### Article 4.3 — Employer Non-Elective Contributions

The Plan Sponsor may, at its discretion, make non-elective Employer Profit-Sharing contributions
to the 401(k) Component for any Plan Year, subject to the overall limit on annual additions set
out in Code § 415(c), which is the lesser of $69,000 (for 2026) or 100% of Compensation. Such
contributions shall be allocated among Participants in proportion to Compensation, unless the Plan
Administrator adopts an alternative allocation formula approved by the Board of Directors.

Non-elective contributions shall vest under the following schedule: 20% after two Years of Service,
40% after three Years of Service, 60% after four Years of Service, 80% after five Years of Service,
and 100% after six or more Years of Service (the "Graded Vesting Schedule"), as permitted under
ERISA § 203(a)(2)(B). Upon termination of employment, a Participant shall be entitled to receive
the vested portion of their account balance. The non-vested portion shall be forfeited and applied
to reduce future Employer contributions or reallocated to eligible Participants.


# PART V — FIDUCIARY RESPONSIBILITIES


### Article 5.1 — General Fiduciary Standards

Each fiduciary of the Plan shall discharge its duties with respect to the Plan solely in the
interest of Participants and Beneficiaries and: (i) for the exclusive purpose of providing benefits
to Participants and Beneficiaries and defraying reasonable expenses of administering the Plan;
(ii) with the care, skill, prudence, and diligence under the circumstances then prevailing that a
prudent person acting in a like capacity and familiar with such matters would use in the conduct of
an enterprise of like character and aims (the "Prudent Person Standard"); (iii) by diversifying the
investments of the Plan so as to minimize the risk of large losses, unless under the circumstances
it is clearly prudent not to do so; and (iv) in accordance with the documents and instruments
governing the Plan insofar as they are consistent with ERISA.

Compliance with the Prudent Person Standard requires each fiduciary to investigate thoroughly all
available options before making an investment or administrative decision, to document its
deliberative process, and to monitor investment managers and other service providers on an ongoing
basis. A fiduciary shall not be relieved of responsibility merely because it delegates authority to
another party, unless such delegation satisfies the requirements of ERISA § 405(c) for proper
delegation and monitoring of investment managers.

The Plan Sponsor shall designate at least one Named Fiduciary with the authority to control and
manage the operation and administration of the Plan. Each Named Fiduciary shall acknowledge its
fiduciary status in writing, shall maintain records of all decisions made in a fiduciary capacity
for at least six years, and shall act consistently with the Investment Policy Statement adopted
pursuant to Article 5.3 hereof. The Benefits Committee shall provide written minutes of each
meeting to the Trustee and shall retain copies of such minutes for not less than six years.


### Article 5.2 — Prohibited Transactions

The Plan shall not engage in a Prohibited Transaction as defined in ERISA § 406 and Code § 4975.
Specifically, the Plan shall not engage, directly or indirectly, in any of the following:

(a) A sale or exchange, or leasing, of any property between the Plan and a Party in Interest;

(b) A lending of money or other extension of credit between the Plan and a Party in Interest;

(c) The furnishing of goods, services, or facilities between the Plan and a Party in Interest;

(d) A transfer to, or use by or for the benefit of, a Party in Interest, of any assets of the Plan;

(e) Acquisition, on behalf of the Plan, of any employer security in excess of the limits imposed
by ERISA § 407, or any employer real property in excess of 10% of the fair market value of Plan
assets; or

(f) Any act by a fiduciary dealing with the income or assets of the Plan in its own interest or
for its own account, or receipt by a fiduciary of any consideration for its own personal account
from any party dealing with the Plan in connection with a transaction involving Plan assets.

Any fiduciary who knowingly participates in a Prohibited Transaction shall be personally liable
under ERISA § 409 for any losses resulting from the breach and shall be required to restore any
profits made through use of Plan assets. The DOL may assess an excise tax equal to 15% of the
amount involved in any Prohibited Transaction under Code § 4975, with an additional 100% excise tax
if the transaction is not corrected within the applicable correction period.


### Article 5.3 — Investment Policy Statement

The Benefits Committee shall adopt and maintain a written Investment Policy Statement ("IPS") in
accordance with ERISA § 402(a)(1). The IPS shall set out: (i) the goals and objectives of the Plan;
(ii) risk tolerance and asset allocation guidelines; (iii) criteria for selection, retention and
removal of investment options; (iv) procedures for monitoring investment performance; and (v)
procedures for assessing fees and expenses paid from Plan assets.

The Benefits Committee shall review and, if appropriate, revise the IPS no less frequently than
annually. Any change to the designated investment options available under the 401(k) Component must
be evaluated against the criteria in the IPS and documented in writing before implementation. The
Benefits Committee shall notify Participants of any change to the investment options at least 30
days before the change takes effect, in accordance with the ERISA § 404(c) regulations to the
extent the Plan is intended to comply with those regulations.

Investment managers selected by the Benefits Committee shall execute written investment management
agreements acknowledging fiduciary status, disclosing all fees, and agreeing to comply with all
applicable provisions of ERISA and the IPS. Each investment manager must provide quarterly
performance reports to the Benefits Committee, including benchmarks against an appropriate index,
and must promptly disclose any material change in its organization, ownership, investment personnel,
or compliance policy that could affect its ability to fulfill its obligations under the agreement.


### Article 5.4 — Fidelity Bond

Each fiduciary and every other person who handles funds or other property of the Plan shall be
bonded in an amount equal to the lesser of (a) 10% of the Plan assets handled or (b) $500,000 (or
$1,000,000 for plans that hold employer securities), pursuant to ERISA § 412. The fidelity bond
shall cover losses caused by acts of fraud or dishonesty on the part of any fiduciary or other
person covered by the bond, directly or through connivance with others. The bond shall be obtained
from a company listed in the U.S. Treasury Department's Circular 570 as an approved surety.

The Plan Administrator shall maintain records confirming that all required fidelity bonds are in
effect and shall promptly notify the DOL if any person who handles Plan funds is not properly
bonded. Failure to maintain the required fidelity bond is a violation of ERISA § 412 and may result
in civil penalties under ERISA § 502(l), including penalties equal to 20% of the applicable
recovery amount ordered by a court or agreed to in settlement.


# PART VI — PLAN ASSETS RULES


### Article 6.1 — Plan Assets Determination

The Plan shall apply DOL Regulation § 2510.3-101 (the "Plan Asset Regulation") to determine whether
assets of an entity in which the Plan invests are themselves "Plan Assets." If the Plan invests in
an entity other than a publicly offered security or a registered investment company, and the Plan's
equity interest in that entity exceeds 25% of any class of equity interests, the underlying assets
of that entity shall be treated as Plan Assets unless the entity qualifies as an operating company
or a registered investment company pursuant to the Plan Asset Regulation.

Where underlying assets of an investee entity are treated as Plan Assets, the managers of that
entity shall themselves be treated as fiduciaries of the Plan and shall be subject to all applicable
provisions of ERISA Part 4, including the prohibitions on Prohibited Transactions under ERISA § 406.
The Benefits Committee shall conduct a pre-investment analysis before authorising any investment
that would make the investee entity's assets Plan Assets, and shall obtain a legal opinion from
qualified ERISA counsel confirming the applicable classification.

The Plan relies on the Qualified Professional Asset Manager ("QPAM") exemption under DOL Prohibited
Transaction Class Exemption 84-14, as amended by PTE 2020-02, to permit transactions that might
otherwise constitute Prohibited Transactions, provided that: (i) the investment manager qualifies
as a QPAM (assets under management of at least $85 million and equity of at least $1 million for
registered investment advisers); (ii) the QPAM negotiates the terms of the transaction at arm's
length; and (iii) the fiduciary with authority to cause the Plan to enter into the transaction does
not have a financial interest in the transaction or the person with whom it was negotiated.


### Article 6.2 — 25% Test and Significant Participation

The Plan Administrator shall monitor investment fund holdings on a quarterly basis to determine
whether the Plan's investment in any entity other than a registered investment company or publicly
offered security exceeds 25% of any class of equity interests issued by that entity. Where any such
threshold is reached or approached, the Benefits Committee shall consult with ERISA counsel and
shall maintain documentation of its analysis and decision in the Plan's fiduciary records.

For purposes of the 25% test, the Plan must aggregate its equity interest with the interests of
each other benefit plan investor (as defined in the Plan Asset Regulation). The Plan Sponsor shall
require representations from investment fund managers regarding the aggregate benefit plan investor
percentage before any investment and on a quarterly basis thereafter. Investment fund managers that
are unable to confirm the aggregate benefit plan investor percentage below 25% shall not be
authorised to receive new capital contributions from the Plan without the written approval of ERISA
counsel to the Plan.


# PART VII — DISTRIBUTIONS AND PAYMENT


### Article 7.1 — Distribution Events and Forms

A Participant shall become entitled to receive a distribution of their vested Account Balance
(401(k) Component) or Accrued Benefit (DB Component) upon the occurrence of any of the following
events: (i) termination of employment; (ii) attainment of Normal Retirement Age; (iii) death;
(iv) Disability; (v) Early Retirement; or (vi) attainment of age 59½ (401(k) Component only).

Distributions from the DB Component shall be made in the form of a qualified joint and survivor
annuity ("QJSA") for married Participants and a single life annuity for unmarried Participants,
unless the Participant (with spousal consent where applicable) elects an alternative optional form
of benefit. Optional forms include lump sum, term-certain annuity, and installment payments, as
set out in Schedule B. Any form of benefit other than the QJSA requires spousal consent that is in
writing, witnessed by a notary public or Plan representative, and obtained within the applicable
election period of 180 days before the annuity starting date.

Mandatory distributions under Code § 401(a)(9) shall be made commencing no later than the
Participant's Required Beginning Date, which is April 1 of the calendar year following the later
of (i) the year in which the Participant attains age 73 (for Participants born after December 31,
1950, pursuant to SECURE Act 2.0) or (ii) the year in which the Participant retires. Required
minimum distributions shall be calculated in accordance with the IRS uniform lifetime table or, if
the Participant's sole designated Beneficiary is a spouse more than 10 years younger, the joint life
and last survivor table.


### Article 7.2 — Hardship Withdrawals

A Participant may receive a hardship withdrawal from their elective deferral account under the
401(k) Component only in the event of an immediate and heavy financial need, as defined under
Treasury Regulation § 1.401(k)-1(d)(3). The following expenses are treated as safe harbor hardship
expenses: (i) medical expenses for the Participant, spouse, dependents or primary beneficiary; (ii)
purchase of the Participant's principal residence; (iii) post-secondary education expenses for the
Participant, spouse, children or primary beneficiary; (iv) prevention of eviction or foreclosure on
the Participant's principal residence; (v) burial or funeral expenses; and (vi) casualty losses.

The amount of a hardship withdrawal shall not exceed the amount of the immediate and heavy financial
need, including amounts necessary to pay reasonably anticipated income taxes and penalties on the
withdrawal. The Participant must represent in writing that the need cannot reasonably be relieved
from other reasonably available resources, including the Participant's own savings, the liquidation
of other assets, or distributions or nontaxable loans from other plans.

Hardship withdrawals are not eligible rollover distributions and are therefore subject to mandatory
20% withholding under Code § 3405(c) and the 10% early withdrawal penalty under Code § 72(t), with
limited exceptions. The Plan Administrator shall maintain records of each hardship withdrawal,
including the Participant's written certification of need and the determination that the withdrawal
satisfies the applicable regulatory requirements.


# PART VIII — CLAIMS AND APPEALS


### Article 8.1 — Claims and Review Procedure

Any Participant or Beneficiary who believes they are entitled to a benefit under the Plan that has
not been paid or has been calculated incorrectly may file a written claim for benefits with the
Plan Administrator. The Plan Administrator shall process all claims in accordance with DOL
Regulation § 2560.503-1 as amended by the ERISA claims and appeals regulations.

For claims relating to disability benefits, the Plan Administrator shall make a determination
within 45 days of receipt of the claim. This period may be extended for up to 30 days if the
extension is necessary due to matters beyond the control of the Plan, provided the claimant is
notified before the end of the initial 45-day period of the circumstances requiring the extension
and the date by which the Plan expects to render a decision. For all other claims, the Plan
Administrator shall make a determination within 90 days of receipt, with extensions permitted
where necessary.

If a claim is denied, the Plan Administrator shall provide the claimant with a written notice that
includes: (i) the specific reason(s) for the denial; (ii) reference to the specific Plan provisions
on which the denial is based; (iii) a description of any additional material or information
necessary for the claimant to perfect the claim and an explanation of why that material is
necessary; and (iv) a description of the Plan's review procedures and the applicable time limits,
including a statement of the claimant's right to bring a civil action under ERISA § 502(a).

A claimant whose claim is denied may appeal the decision within 60 days following receipt of the
denial notice. The appeal shall be heard by the Benefits Committee, which has not been involved in
the initial claim review. The Benefits Committee shall take into account all comments, documents,
records, and other information submitted by the claimant without regard to whether such information
was submitted or considered previously. The Benefits Committee shall render a final determination
within 60 days of receipt of the appeal, or within 120 days if special circumstances require an
extension. Any final determination shall describe the claimant's right to a civil action under
ERISA § 502(a)(1)(B).


# PART IX — REPORTING AND DISCLOSURE


### Article 9.1 — Annual Report and Disclosure Requirements

The Plan Administrator shall cause the Plan to comply with all reporting and disclosure requirements
under ERISA Title I, Part 1. Specifically, the Plan Administrator shall:

(a) File an Annual Report on Form 5500 (including Schedule SB for the DB Component and Schedule H
for the 401(k) Component where applicable) with the DOL within 7 months following the end of the
Plan Year, or within 8.5 months if a timely extension is filed using Form 5558;

(b) Distribute a Summary Plan Description ("SPD") to all Plan Participants and Beneficiaries within
90 days of becoming a Participant and within 120 days of the Plan's establishment, describing in
plain language all material terms of the Plan, including eligibility, participation, vesting,
contribution formulas, distribution provisions, and claims procedures;

(c) Distribute a Summary of Material Modifications ("SMM") within 210 days after the end of any
Plan Year in which a material modification is adopted, or within 60 days if the modification is a
material reduction in benefits or covered services, in accordance with DOL Regulation § 2520.104b-3;

(d) Comply with the automatic enrollment notice requirements of Code § 414(w), providing initial
and annual notices to all Participants describing their elective deferral rights and the default

# PART X — PLAN AMENDMENT AND TERMINATION


### Article 10.1 — Amendment

The Plan Sponsor reserves the right to amend the Plan at any time and from time to time, provided

### Article 10.2 — Plan Termination

The Plan Sponsor may terminate the Plan at any time, subject to the requirements of ERISA § 4041
for single-employer defined benefit plans and the vesting requirements of ERISA § 203(e) for all
components. Upon termination of either component of the Plan, the Accrued Benefits of all affected
Participants shall become fully vested and non-forfeitable, regardless of the Participants' number
of Years of Service.

Upon termination of the DB Component, the Plan Sponsor shall notify the Pension Benefit Guaranty
Corporation ("PBGC") at least 60 days in advance if the termination is a standard termination, or
as soon as administratively feasible if the termination is a distress termination, in accordance
with ERISA §§ 4041 and 4042. A standard termination requires that the Plan have sufficient assets
to purchase annuity contracts from an insurance company for the benefit of all Participants and
Beneficiaries, on or before the final distribution date.

The assets of the Trust shall be applied first to provide all benefits due to Participants and
Beneficiaries, including purchase of annuity contracts where required, before any reversion to the
Plan Sponsor. A reversion of residual assets to the Plan Sponsor is permissible only if the Plan
expressly permits it, the Plan has no underfunded benefit liabilities, and an excise tax of 20–50%

# SCHEDULE A — 401(k) COMPONENT SPECIFIC PROVISIONS

### A.1 — Self-Directed Brokerage Account

Participants may direct a portion of their Account Balance into a self-directed brokerage account
("SDBA") through the Plan's designated brokerage provider, subject to a minimum balance of $5,000
in the core investment menu at all times. Investments through the SDBA are deemed individually
directed by the Participant and are excluded from ERISA § 404(c) protection with respect to the
Participant's investment in any security that is not part of the designated investment menu. The
Benefits Committee shall ensure that the SDBA provider complies with applicable securities laws and
complies with the 408(b)(2) disclosure requirements.


### A.2 — Loan Provisions

Participants may borrow from their 401(k) Component Account Balance, subject to the following
requirements: (i) the maximum loan amount shall not exceed the lesser of $50,000 (reduced by the
highest outstanding loan balance in the previous 12 months) or 50% of the Participant's vested
account balance; (ii) the loan shall be repayable within five years through payroll deductions,
except for loans used to purchase the Participant's principal residence which may be repaid over a
period not exceeding 25 years; (iii) the loan shall bear interest at a rate equal to the prime
rate published in The Wall Street Journal on the first business day of the month in which the loan
is made, plus 1%; and (iv) only one loan per Participant shall be outstanding at any time.

A loan that fails to meet the requirements of Code § 72(p) shall be treated as a taxable
distribution in the year of default, subject to income tax and the 10% early withdrawal penalty
under Code § 72(t). The Plan Administrator shall maintain records of all outstanding loans,
including terms, repayment schedules, and any defaults, for at least six years following repayment.


# SCHEDULE B — DEFINED BENEFIT COMPONENT SPECIFIC PROVISIONS

### B.1 — Benefit Formula

The annual Normal Retirement Benefit for each Participant under the DB Component shall be equal to
the sum of: (i) 1.5% of average Annual Compensation for the five consecutive Plan Years of highest
Compensation ("Final Average Compensation") multiplied by Years of Benefit Service earned before
January 1, 2010; plus (ii) 1.25% of Final Average Compensation multiplied by Years of Benefit
Service earned on and after January 1, 2010, subject to a maximum of 40 Years of Benefit Service
for purposes of the formula.

The annual benefit provided under the benefit formula above shall not exceed the limitations of
Code § 415(b), which is the lesser of: (i) $275,000 (for 2026), indexed to the cost of living; or
(ii) 100% of the Participant's average Compensation for the three consecutive highest-paid years.
The Plan Administrator shall calculate and apply the Code § 415 limits on each annuity starting
date and shall apply the required adjustments for payment forms other than straight life annuities.

### B.2 — Minimum Funding

The Plan Sponsor shall make contributions to the DB Component sufficient to meet the minimum
funding requirements of Code § 412 and ERISA § 302, as calculated by the Plan's enrolled actuary
using the funding method and actuarial assumptions adopted by the Benefits Committee. The funding
method shall not be changed without IRS approval pursuant to Code § 412(d)(1). The most recent
actuarial report shall be made available to each Named Fiduciary and shall be filed with the DOL as
part of the Form 5500 Annual Report.

A funding shortfall shall be corrected by the Plan Sponsor within the time limits prescribed by
Code § 412(e), which generally requires the shortfall to be amortized over seven years in level
annual installments. If the Plan Sponsor fails to make required minimum contributions, the PBGC
lien provisions of ERISA § 302(f) shall apply, and the DOL may assess a penalty of 10% of the
unpaid contribution, with an additional 100% penalty for contributions that remain unpaid after a
Tax Court decision, under Code § 4971.
